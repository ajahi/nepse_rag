# NEPSE Knowledge Base (basic)

## What is NEPSE
NEPSE (Nepal Stock Exchange) is the only stock exchange of Nepal. It is located
in Kathmandu and provides a marketplace for trading listed securities such as
ordinary shares, preference shares, debentures, and government bonds. It operates
under the regulation of the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON).

## NEPSE Index
The NEPSE Index is the main benchmark index of the Nepal Stock Exchange. It is a
market-capitalization-weighted index that reflects the overall performance of all
listed companies. When people say "the market went up," they usually mean the
NEPSE Index rose. There are also sub-indices for sectors and a float index
(NEPSE Float Index) based only on shares available for public trading.

## Trading hours and days
The NEPSE market normally trades Sunday through Thursday. Friday and Saturday are
weekly holidays, along with public holidays. Continuous trading typically runs in
the late morning to early afternoon; pre-open and closing sessions bracket it.
Because of this, there is no price data on Fridays, Saturdays, or holidays.

## Sectors
Listed companies are grouped into sectors, including: Commercial Banks,
Development Banks, Finance, Microfinance, Life Insurance, Non-Life Insurance,
Hydropower, Hotels and Tourism, Manufacturing and Processing, Trading,
Investment, and Others. Commercial banks and hydropower companies make up a large
share of market activity.

## Key terms
- Close price: the last traded price of a stock on a given trading day.
- Open / High / Low: the first, highest, and lowest traded prices of the day.
- Volume: the number of shares traded in a day.
- Turnover: the total rupee value traded (roughly price times volume).
- Market capitalization: share price multiplied by the number of listed shares;
  a measure of a company's total market value.
- Number of trades: how many individual transactions occurred in a stock.
- LTP (Last Traded Price): the most recent price at which a stock traded.

## How a yearly gain is calculated
The yearly gain of a stock is (last close of the year − first close of the year)
divided by the first close, expressed as a percent. The first and last closes
must fall within that stock's real trading history — a stock listed mid-year has
no earlier data.

## Circuit breakers and price bands
NEPSE applies daily price limits (circuit breakers) that cap how far a single
stock's price can move in one day (commonly around 10% up or down from the
previous close). Market-wide circuit breakers can halt trading if the index moves
sharply.

## Regulator
SEBON (Securities Board of Nepal) is the regulator that oversees the securities
market, licenses intermediaries, and protects investors. CDSC (CDS and Clearing
Limited) handles dematerialized (demat) shares, clearing, and settlement.

## Currency
All prices and turnover on NEPSE are in Nepalese Rupees (NPR).

## Data coverage note
Not every listed company has the same history. A ticker listed later has no
price data before its listing date, so questions about periods before a stock
existed cannot be answered from its data.
